Family Task Management Guide

This document describes how the Kaplan family shared task system works. It
covers who can create tasks, what information a task must include, how
reminders are scheduled, and how tasks move from open to done.

1. Who can create a task

Any registered family member may create a shared task. There is no separate
"admin" role — every family member has equal permission to add, edit, or
close a task. This matches the household's general trust model: tasks are a
shared responsibility, not something only parents assign to children.

A task may also be created on behalf of another family member. For example,
a parent can create a task and immediately assign it to a child, or one
sibling can create a task and assign it to another. The person who creates
the task is recorded as the creator, separately from the person marked as
responsible for completing it.

2. Required fields on every task

Every task in the system must have the following four pieces of
information before it can be saved:

- Title: a short one-line summary of the task, for example "Take out the
  recycling" or "Buy a birthday present for Grandma."
- Description: a longer free-text explanation of what needs to be done, any
  relevant details, and where to find supplies if needed. The description
  can be left empty for very simple tasks, but the title is always
  required.
- Due date: the date by which the task should be completed. Tasks without a
  specific deadline should still get a due date — if there is truly no
  deadline, the creator should pick a reasonable target date rather than
  leave the field blank, since the reminder system depends on it.
  Recurring tasks are not supported yet; a weekly task like "take out the
  trash" must currently be re-created each week.
- Responsible person: exactly one family member who owns the task. Tasks
  cannot be assigned to more than one person at a time. If a task genuinely
  requires two people, it should be split into two separate tasks, one per
  person.

3. Reminder timing

Every task has a reminder setting, chosen by whoever creates the task. The
system supports the following reminder options:

- Same day: a reminder is sent on the morning of the due date itself.
- One day before: a reminder is sent the evening before the due date. This
  is the default option if the creator does not choose one explicitly.
- Three days before: recommended for tasks that require some preparation
  or shopping, such as buying a gift or booking an appointment.
- One week before: recommended for larger tasks, such as planning a family
  trip or scheduling a doctor's visit.

Reminders are sent as a single notification at the chosen time; the system
does not currently send repeated or escalating reminders if a task is
ignored. If a task passes its due date without being marked done, it is
shown as "overdue" in the task list, but no additional reminder is sent
automatically.

4. Marking a task done

When a task is created, the creator chooses who is responsible for it --
either themselves, or another family member. Either the responsible
person or the creator may mark the task as done, even when they are two
different people. Other family members, who are neither the creator nor
the responsible person, can view the task and its status, but cannot
close it themselves. This is meant to prevent a task from being marked
complete by someone who didn't actually do the work.

Once a task is marked done, it moves out of the active task list and into
a "completed" history view. Completed tasks are kept for the current
season only; older completed tasks are periodically cleared to keep the
list manageable.

5. Editing and deleting tasks

Any of the four required fields — title, description, due date, or
responsible person — can be edited after a task is created, by either the
creator or the responsible person. Editing the responsible person is
effectively reassigning the task to someone else; the system keeps no
separate history of previous assignees, only the current one.

Deleting a task removes it permanently, including its reminder schedule.
There is no "trash" or undo for deleted tasks, so deletion should be used
carefully, and mainly for tasks that were created by mistake or are no
longer relevant at all — a task that is simply not needed anymore because
priorities changed should usually be marked done or edited rather than
deleted, so there's still a record of what was decided.

6. Finding tasks from email (planned feature)

A planned extension to this system will scan a family member's email inbox
for messages that look like they describe an actionable task — for
example, a school notice asking a parent to sign and return a form by a
certain date, or a reminder from a doctor's office about an upcoming
appointment. The system will suggest these as draft tasks, pre-filling the
title and due date where possible, but a human will always need to review
and confirm a suggested task before it becomes a real one; nothing is
added to the shared list automatically from email alone.

7. Notifications and quiet hours

Reminders are not sent between 9 PM and 7 AM local time, regardless of
what reminder timing was chosen for a task. If a reminder would otherwise
fire during those hours, it is delayed until 7 AM the same day it would
have gone out, or the following morning if the scheduled time already
falls after 9 PM.

8. Household scope

The task system is meant for household and family-coordination tasks:
chores, errands, appointments, school-related items, and similar shared
responsibilities. It is not intended for tracking work projects,
schoolwork grades, or financial budgeting — those are considered out of
scope for this tool and are expected to be handled with other,
purpose-built tools instead.
